\section{The problem and the idea}

We want $\braket{\Psi_1|\Psi_2}$ for two CASSCF wavefunctions whose orbitals
were optimized independently: two states from separate optimizations, or the
same state at two geometries. The orbital sets are individually orthonormal
but not orthonormal to each other.

Done directly, this is a double sum over determinant pairs, each term a
L\"owdin determinant of mutual spin-orbital overlaps,\cite{Loewdin1955,Plasser2016}
\begin{equation}
 \braket{\Psi_1|\Psi_2}
 = \sum_{KL} c_1(K)^{*} c_2(L) \braket{\Phi_K|\Phi'_L},
 \qquad
 \braket{\Phi_K|\Phi'_L} = \det \mathbf{S}^{KL},
 \label{eq:naive}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{S}^{KL} = \mathbf{C}_K^{\dagger}\mathbf{S}_{XY}\mathbf{C}_L,
\end{equation}
where $X$ and $Y$ are AO bases for $\Phi_K$ and $\Phi'_L$ respectively.
This costs $O(n_{\det,1} n_{\det,2})$ determinant evaluations. Plasser
\emph{et al.}\cite{Plasser2016} attack this head-on, by never forming most of
those determinants twice.

Malmqvist\cite{Malmqvist1986} takes the other route, and it is the one
implemented here. If the two orbital sets can be transformed to a
\emph{biorthonormal} pair, \textit{i.e.}, $\varphi^A = \varphi^X C^{XA}$,
$\varphi^B = \varphi^Y C^{YB}$ with $(C^{XA})^\dagger S^{XY} C^{YB} = 1$,
then every mutual overlap matrix in \cref{eq:naive} becomes an identity block,
each determinant overlap collapses to $\delta_{KL}$, and the whole double sum
becomes a single dot product of the two re-expressed CI vectors:
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\;\braket{\Psi_1|\Psi_2} = c_1'^\dagger c_2'\;}
 \label{eq:payoff}
\end{equation}
(Malmqvist Eq.~(11), p.~492). The cost moves entirely into re-expressing each
CI vector in its new basis, which is a handful of sigma-vector builds.

The rest of this note is about making \cref{eq:payoff} concrete: how to build
the transformation (\cref{sec:biorth}), what it does to a CI vector
(\cref{sec:ci}), how it is applied in practice (\cref{sec:impl}), and the one
numerical pitfall that matters more than all the others put together
(\cref{sec:cores}).

\section{Notation}

$X$ and $Y$ are the two orbital sets, sharing a partition into $n_{\mathbf{C}}$ core
(``docc'') and $n_{\mathbf{A}}$ active orbitals.
The virtual orbitals are never occupied and can be left out of consideration.
$K,L$ index determinants; $\mathbf{C},\mathbf{A}$ always label the core and active blocks. The
mixed MO overlap is
\begin{equation}
 S^{XY} = (C^X)^\dagger S_\mathrm{AO}(X,Y)\, C^Y
 = \begin{pmatrix} S_{\mathbf{C}\mathbf{C}} & S_{\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}} \\ S_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}} & S_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}} \end{pmatrix},
 \label{eq:mixed-overlap}
\end{equation}
with $S_\mathrm{AO}(X,Y)$ the AO overlap between the (possibly distinct)
geometries or basis sets behind $X$ and $Y$.

\section{Building the biorthonormal pair}
\label{sec:biorth}

Not every transformation is allowed. The CAS expansion is closed only under
the subgroup whose active-row/docc-column block vanishes (Malmqvist Sec.~V,
Fig.~1): new core orbitals must be pure recombinations of old core
orbitals, while new active orbitals may absorb core character. Both
$C^{XA}$ and $C^{YB}$ are built block upper-triangular for this reason.

\texttt{biorthogonalize\_casscf\_orbitals} follows Malmqvist's Appendix,
Eqs.~(A.1)--(A.7):

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{(A.2)} SVD the core block, $S_{\mathbf{C}\mathbf{C}}=U_{\mathbf{C}} D_{\mathbf{C}} V_{\mathbf{C}}^\dagger$.
If any singular value $d_{\mathbf{C},i}$ is near-zero, then the no biorthonormal pair exists within the CAS-closed group.
\item \textbf{(A.3)} Remove core admixture from the active block,
$S'_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}} = S_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}} - S_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}} V_{\mathbf{C}} D_{\mathbf{C}}^{-1} U_{\mathbf{C}}^\dagger S_{\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}}$.
This is the generalized Schur's complement of $S_{\mathbf{CC}}$ in $S^{XY}$, \textit{i.e.}, the result of removing the couplings to the core overlap.
\item \textbf{(A.4)} SVD it, $S'_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}}=U_{\mathbf{A}} D_{\mathbf{A}} V_{\mathbf{A}}^\dagger$.
\item \textbf{(A.4$'$, A.4$''$, A.5)} Rotate each side onto its own orthonormal
``pseudo-corresponding'' basis. Writing $C^X_{\mathbf{C}}$, $C^X_{\mathbf{A}}$ for the
core and active columns of the AO-basis MO coefficient matrix $C^X$ from
\cref{eq:mixed-overlap} (and likewise for $Y$),
\begin{equation}
 C^{P_1}_{\mathbf{C}} = C^{X}_{\mathbf{C}} U_{\mathbf{C}}, \quad
 C^{P_1}_{\mathbf{A}} = C^{X}_{\mathbf{A}} U_{\mathbf{A}}
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 C^{P_2}_{\mathbf{C}} = C^{Y}_{\mathbf{C}} V_{\mathbf{C}}, \quad
 C^{P_2}_{\mathbf{A}} = C^{Y}_{\mathbf{A}} V_{\mathbf{A}}.
\end{equation}
The new overlap in the pseudo-corresponding orbital bases is given by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pcovlp}
 S^{P_1P_2} = \begin{pmatrix}
 D_{\mathbf{C}} & S^{P_1P_2}_{\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}} \\
 S^{P_1P_2}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}} & D_{\mathbf{A}} + S^{P_1P_2}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}} D_{\mathbf{C}}^{-1} S^{P_1P_2}_{\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}}
 \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
diagonal only within the core block.
\item \textbf{(A.6$'$, A.6$''$)} Shift to the nonorthogonal bases $A$ and $B$: each side's
core columns are carried over unchanged, and each side's active columns absorb
a linear combination of core orbitals designed to zero out the off-diagonal overlap
in \cref{eq:pcovlp}:
\begin{equation}
 C^{A}_{\mathbf{C}} \equiv C^{P_1}_{\mathbf{C}}, \qquad
 C^{A}_{\mathbf{A}} = C^{P_1}_{\mathbf{A}} - C^{P_1}_{\mathbf{C}} D_{\mathbf{C}}^{-1}
 \big(S^{P_1P_2}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}}\big)^\dagger,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 C^{B}_{\mathbf{C}} \equiv C^{P_2}_{\mathbf{C}}, \qquad
 C^{B}_{\mathbf{A}} = C^{P_2}_{\mathbf{A}} - C^{P_2}_{\mathbf{C}} D_{\mathbf{C}}^{-1}
 S^{P_1P_2}_{\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}}.
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{(A.7)} In this nonorthogonal basis the overlap is diagonal but not
yet identity,
\begin{equation}
 S^{AB} = \begin{pmatrix} D_{\mathbf{C}} & 0 \\ 0 & D_{\mathbf{A}} \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Rescale the $B$ side by $d=(D_{\mathbf{C}},D_{\mathbf{A}})$, giving
$(C^{XA})^\dagger S^{XY} C^{YB}=1$ exactly.
\end{enumerate}

Composing all six steps gives the explicit block form for the nonorthogonal transformations $C^{XA}$ and $C^{YB}$:
\begin{equation}
 C^{XA} = \begin{pmatrix}
 U_{\mathbf{C}} & -U_{\mathbf{C}} D_{\mathbf{C}}^{-1}\big(S^{P_1P_2}_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{C}}\big)^\dagger \\
 0 & U_{\mathbf{A}}
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 C^{YB} = \begin{pmatrix}
 V_{\mathbf{C}} D_{\mathbf{C}}^{-1} & -V_{\mathbf{C}} D_{\mathbf{C}}^{-1} S^{P_1P_2}_{\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}} D_{\mathbf{A}}^{-1} \\
 0 & V_{\mathbf{A}} D_{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}
 \end{pmatrix},
 \label{eq:cxa-cyb}
\end{equation}
each block upper-triangular in the (core, active) ordering, as promised at the
start of this section. Only the $B$ side carries the extra $D_{\mathbf{C}}^{-1}$,
$D_{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}$ column rescale from step (A.7); the $A$ side is left as
assembled in step (A.6$'$).

It is worth noting that step 5 does not change the CI vector at all.
Adding a multiple of an always-fully-occupied orbital to
another occupied orbital leaves a Slater determinant unchanged. 
So only steps 4 and 6 touch the CI coefficients, and the
active-space work reduces to \emph{one orthogonal rotation and one diagonal
rescale}. (This is only the active-active piece: the core block contributes
its own separate nonorthogonal factor, the scalar of \cref{eq:docc-scalar}, so
$C^{XA}$ and $C^{YB}$ are nonorthogonal overall) 
That fact drives the implementation in \cref{sec:impl}.

The SVDs are unique only up to rotations within degenerate singular subspaces,
so $C^{XA}$ and $C^{YB}$ can come back far from the identity even for nearly
identical orbital sets. This is a valid solution, not an error, and the
CI-vector transform is built to tolerate large rotation angles.

\section{What the transformation does to a CI vector}
\label{sec:ci}

For an invertible orbital transformation $M$ such that
$\varphi^\mathrm{new}=\varphi^\mathrm{old} M$, the operator $\hat{\rho}$ that delivers
a CI vector expanded in the old basis in the new basis can be written as an
exponentiated one-body Fock-space operator:\cite{MoshinskySeligman1971}
\begin{equation}
 \hat{\rho}[M] = \exp\!\Big(-\sum_{pq}(\log M)_{pq}\,\hat{E}_{pq}\Big),
 \label{eq:rho-def}
\end{equation}
with $E_{pq}=p_\alpha^\dagger q_\alpha+p_\beta^\dagger q_\beta$
in the nonrelativistic case, and $E_{pq}=p^\dagger q$ for the two-component case
(\cref{sec:twocomp}).
Crucially, $\Psi$ itself does not change under this transformation --- only the
expansion does. $\hat{\rho}[M]$ acts on the \emph{coefficient vector}, mapping
the old-basis coefficients of $\Psi$ to its new-basis coefficients, for the
same fixed $\Psi$ and the same determinant index $K$ on both sides
(Malmqvist's Eqs.~(32)--(34)):
\begin{equation}
 \ket{\Psi} = \sum_K c_K\ket{\Phi_K} = \sum_K c'_K\ket{\Phi'_K},
 \qquad c' = \hat{\rho}[M]\,c.
\end{equation}
Because orbitals transform by \emph{right} multiplication, composition reverses on the CI side:
\begin{equation}
 \hat{\rho}[M_1M_2] = \hat{\rho}[M_2]\,\hat{\rho}[M_1].
 \label{eq:anti}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Where this form comes from, informally.} $\log M$ is playing exactly
the role of the familiar MCSCF rotation generator $\kappa$
($\varphi^\mathrm{new}=\varphi^\mathrm{old}\exp(\kappa)$) --- just for a
possibly large, nonunitary rotation instead of an infinitesimal unitary one;
$t=\log M$ is simply ``the $\kappa$ that produces this particular $M$.'' The
one-body operator $\sum_{pq}t_{pq}\hat{E}_{pq}$, built from those same
numbers, is the standard generator that reshuffles CI amplitude between
determinants related by that orbital rotation.

The minus sign is the same one already present in MCSCF orbital-gradient
theory: rotating the \emph{orbitals} by $t$ rotates the \emph{determinants}
by $+t$ too ($\ket{\Phi'}=\exp(\hat{t})\ket{\Phi}$, directly from
$a_p'^\dagger=\sum_q M_{qp}a_q^\dagger$), but the \emph{coefficients} of a
fixed wavefunction $\Psi=\sum_K c_K\ket{\Phi_K}=\sum_K c'_K\ket{\Phi'_K}$ must
move the opposite way to compensate, so $c'=\exp(-\hat{t})\,c$.

\subsection{The core block is a scalar}

$M$ is block upper-triangular, and for such a matrix any analytic function,
including $\log$, is block upper-triangular with the same zero block and
with $(\log M)_{\mathbf{C}\mathbf{C}} = \log (M_{\mathbf{C}\mathbf{C}})$. 
Two consequences, given that every
determinant in a CAS expansion has the core block completely full:

\begin{itemize}
\item The zero $(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{C})$ block means the generator never promotes an electron
out of the core space, so the transformed wavefunction stays inside the
same CAS manifold. This is exactly the closure property of Malmqvist's
Sec.~V subgroup.
\item Every off-diagonal one-body term with a core creation index will have 
no effect on the wavefunction, only the diagonal number operators survive. 
The nonzero $(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{A})$ block belongs to this class as well.

\end{itemize}

So the entire core block contributes one state-independent scalar,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\rho[M]\big|_{\mathbf{C}}&=\exp\!\Big(-\sum_{p\in \mathbf{C}} t_{pp}\, n_p\Big)\\
&=\exp\left(-n_{\mathrm{ch}}\sum_{p\in\mathbf{C}}(\log M)_{pp}\right)\\
&=\exp\left(-n_{\mathrm{ch}}\Tr(\log{M}_{\mathbf{CC}})\right)\\
&=\exp\left(-n_{\mathrm{ch}}\log(\det{M}_{\mathbf{CC}})\right)\\
& =\det(M_{\mathbf{C}\mathbf{C}})^{-n_\mathrm{ch}},
 \label{eq:docc-scalar}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $n_\mathrm{ch}$ counts occupied second-quantized channels per core
orbital: $n_\mathrm{ch}=2$ nonrelativistically (a doubly occupied spatial
orbital contributes $\det(M_{\mathbf{C}\mathbf{C}})^{-1}$ per spin), and $n_\mathrm{ch}=1$ for
singly occupied spinors. This reproduces Malmqvist's factor
$\prod_i (t_{ii})^2$ (p.~489) and the explicit factor of 2 in his Eq.~(10),
p.~492.

Only the active--active block needs a genuine generator.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:impl}

\subsection{Use the factors, not their product}

The active block of $C^{XA}$ is \emph{exactly} the orthogonal factor $U_{\mathbf{A}}$,
and that of $C^{YB}$ is \emph{exactly} $V_{\mathbf{A}} D_{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}$. Rather than
multiplying these together and taking a matrix logarithm of the product, the
implementation keeps them --- \texttt{biorthogonalize\_casscf\_orbitals}
returns a \texttt{BiorthogonalOrbitals} carrying \texttt{U\_actv\_A},
\texttt{U\_actv\_B} and \texttt{d\_actv} alongside the assembled matrices ---
and applies them as two exact steps. Per \cref{eq:anti}, the rotation is
applied to the CI vector first and the rescale second:
\begin{equation}
 \rho[V_{\mathbf{A}} D_{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}] = \rho[D_{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}]\,\rho[V_{\mathbf{A}}].
\end{equation}

This matters for two reasons. First, the diagonal rescale then has a closed
form --- Malmqvist's Appendix step 6 --- costing one pass over the determinant
list:
\begin{equation}
 \rho[D_{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}]\,c(\mu) = c(\mu) \prod_t d_t^{\,n_t^\mu},
 \label{eq:diag-closed}
\end{equation}
with $n_t^\mu$ the occupation of active orbital $t$ in determinant $\mu$.
Routing it through \cref{eq:rho-def} instead would demand a Taylor series
whose length grows with the spread of $d_{\mathbf{A}}$ --- the worst-conditioned piece of
the whole transformation, and entirely avoidable. Second, the only matrix
logarithm left is that of an orthogonal matrix, whose norm is bounded by
$\pi$.

\subsection{Improper rotations}

$U_{\mathbf{A}}$ and $V_{\mathbf{A}}$ are orthogonal but may be \emph{improper}
($\det = -1$), which has no real logarithm at all, since
$\det(\exp\kappa)=\exp(\Tr\kappa)>0$ for real $\kappa$. Such a factor is split
as $U = FR$ with $F$ flipping the sign of active orbital 0 and $R$ is a proper rotation.
$F$'s CI action is exact: multiply each coefficient by
$(-1)^{n_0}$, and $R$ is logged from its real Schur form, reading each
$2\times2$ block's angle with \texttt{arctan2}. That is branch-free over the
full range, unlike \texttt{scipy.linalg.logm}, whose principal branch returns
a spuriously complex result for rotations near $\pi$. By \cref{eq:anti} the
reflection is applied before $R$'s generator.

\subsection{Applying the generator}

The generator here lives entirely in the active space: $M$ is one of
$U_{\mathbf{A}}$, $V_{\mathbf{A}}$, or --- after the improper-rotation split above
--- the proper factor $R$, each an $n_{\mathbf{A}}\times n_{\mathbf{A}}$ matrix, and
$t=\log M$ is its generator (real for the nonrelativistic case, complex for
two-component). Restricting \cref{eq:rho-def} to the active block,
\begin{equation}
 \hat{\rho}[M] = \exp(-\hat{T}), \qquad
 \hat{T} = \sum_{p,q\in\mathbf{A}} t_{pq}\hat{E}_{pq}.
 \label{eq:that-def}
\end{equation}

\textbf{The one-electron sigma build, concretely.} Acting on a CI vector with
coefficients $c_\nu$ in the determinant basis, $\hat{T}$'s matrix elements are
exactly Malmqvist's one-electron coupling coefficients:
\begin{equation}
 (\hat{T}c)_\mu = \sum_\nu T_{\mu\nu}\,c_\nu,
 \qquad T_{\mu\nu} = \sum_{p,q\in\mathbf{A}} t_{pq}\,A^{\mu\nu}_{pq},
 \qquad A^{\mu\nu}_{pq} = \braket{\Phi_\mu|\hat{E}_{pq}|\Phi_\nu}.
\end{equation}
This is exactly the quantity every direct-CI one-electron sigma build already
contracts against a Hamiltonian's one-electron integrals ---
\texttt{sigma\_one\_electron} computes precisely this contraction,
string-by-string, without ever forming the dense $n_{\det}\times n_{\det}$
matrix $T_{\mu\nu}$. Calling it with $t$ in place of one-electron integrals
and $V=0$ (so \texttt{sigma\_two\_electron}'s contribution is skipped
entirely) is therefore exact, not an approximation: $\hat{T}$ genuinely is a
one-body operator, so no two-electron machinery is needed no matter how large
$t$ is.

\textbf{Scaling-and-squaring.} \texttt{transform\_ci\_vector\_direct}
evaluates $\exp(-\hat{T})c$ by first halving the generator $m$ times, to
$t_m = t/2^m$, until $\lVert t_m\rVert_2$ falls below a threshold. The
resulting well-conditioned small-angle exponential is then built as a Taylor
series, recursively and exactly matching the code:
\begin{equation}
 c^{(0)} = c, \qquad
 c^{(k)} = -\frac{1}{k}\,\hat{T}_m\,c^{(k-1)}
 \;\Big(=\tfrac{(-1)^k}{k!}\hat{T}_m^{\,k}c\Big),
 \qquad
 \exp(-\hat{T}_m)\,c \approx \sum_{k=0}^{K} c^{(k)},
\end{equation}
with $\hat{T}_m=\sum_{pq}(t_m)_{pq}\hat{E}_{pq}$ --- one
\texttt{sigma\_one\_electron} call per term $c^{(k)}$, summed until $c^{(k)}$
is negligible relative to the running total. That converged small-angle
result is then applied $2^m$ times in sequence, which is exact since
$\exp(-\hat{T})=\big(\exp(-\hat{T}_m)\big)^{2^m}$.

Note that this is not Malmqvist's
own recommendation: his Sec.~IV gives an exact finite algorithm via an LU
partitioning and a sequence of single-orbital transformations, costing
$O(n_{\mathbf{A}})$ operations with no logarithm anywhere. The exponential route is
chosen here for simplicity, and is a reasonable trade only because
\cref{eq:diag-closed} removes the ill-conditioned part.

\subsection{Two-component specialization}
\label{sec:twocomp}

Spinors carry both spin components internally, so there is no $\alpha/\beta$
pairing; \texttt{RelCISigmaBuilder} packs all active electrons into a single
string. Four things change:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Single-channel generator}, $E_{pq}=p^\dagger q$.
\item \textbf{$n_\mathrm{ch}=1$} in \cref{eq:docc-scalar}, since core
spinors are singly occupied. Getting this wrong returns a plausible but wrong
number rather than failing, so it is pinned by a dedicated test
(\cref{sec:verify}).
\item \textbf{No branch-cut problem.} $\exp\colon\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb C)
\to GL(n,\mathbb C)$ is surjective, so every invertible complex matrix has a
logarithm; \texttt{logm} is applied directly with no reflection handling. (The
real obstruction above is visible in $\det(\exp\kappa)>0$, but that condition
alone is necessary, not sufficient --- surjectivity follows from the Jordan
form, every eigenvalue being nonzero and admitting a complex logarithm.)
Verified numerically: even for a matrix with an eigenvalue exactly at $-1$,
$\exp(\log U)=U$ holds to $2\times10^{-15}$.
\item \textbf{Spin-doubled AO overlap.} Forte2 stores spinor coefficients as
$\binom{C^\alpha}{C^\beta}$, so \cref{eq:mixed-overlap} needs
$S_\mathrm{AO}\to\diag(S_\mathrm{AO},S_\mathrm{AO})$. \texttt{mo\_overlap}
applies \texttt{block\_diag\_2x2} whenever the system is two-component,
matching \texttt{System.ints\_overlap()}.
\end{enumerate}

\texttt{casscf\_wavefunction\_overlap} reads this off
\texttt{system\_1.two\_component} and rejects a mismatched pair, rather than
taking a flag that could disagree with the orbitals passed in.

\section{Tight-core orbitals: the dominant numerical pitfall}
\label{sec:cores}

For overlaps between different geometries, the largest error source is not the
algorithm --- it is the tightest core orbitals. Tight cores follow their nuclei, so
their mutual overlap decays steeply with displacement, and that decay
multiplies straight into \cref{eq:payoff}. Plasser \emph{et al.}\ document this
in their Sec.~3.3.

Rigidly translating a molecule leaves its wavefunction physically unchanged,
so the overlap ought to be 1. Measured with this implementation (STO-3G,
overlap of an identical state against its own translate):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
& $n_\mathrm{core}$ & 0.05 bohr & 0.1 bohr & 0.2 bohr \\
\midrule
LiH & 1 & 0.9935 & 0.9744 & 0.9030 \\
FH  & 4 & 0.9288 & 0.7532 & 0.3585 \\
ClH & 8 & 0.7407 & 0.3342 & 0.0256 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The artifact grows sharply with nuclear charge --- for HCl a displacement of
0.1~\AA{} destroys the overlap entirely. This is worst exactly where the
two-component path is used, since X2C is reached for precisely the heavy
elements with the tightest cores.

The mitigation, following Plasser's Eq.~(21), is to discard the tightest core
orbitals from the overlap, assuming they are orthonormal between the two
states and orthogonal to everything retained. \texttt{casscf\_wavefunction\_overlap}
exposes this as \texttt{n\_frozen\_docc}. For FH displaced by 0.1 bohr,
freezing the 1s lifts the overlap from 0.75 to 0.94.

\textbf{Freeze only tight cores.} The assumption fails for valence orbitals,
and over-freezing is worse than not freezing at all: for the same FH case,
freezing the entire core block (all four orbitals) gives 0.05. The two halves
of the assumption behave very differently, and only one is diagnostic:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.42\linewidth}p{0.50\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Assumption & Behavior \\
\midrule
Frozen orbitals mutually orthonormal & Fails by design whenever nuclei move;
removing this decay is the \emph{point} of freezing. Not checked. \\
Frozen orthogonal to retained orbitals & Fails only when freezing is genuinely
invalid. Checked, with a warning. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For FH at 0.1 bohr the frozen--retained coupling is $0.09$ when freezing the 1s
(fine) and $0.97$ when freezing all four (invalid), so the check separates the
two cases cleanly.

\section{Verification}
\label{sec:verify}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.34\linewidth}p{0.58\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Test & What it pins down \\
\midrule

\end{center}

A physical check outside the automated suite: scanning
$\braket{\Psi(R_e)|\Psi(R)}$ for LiH out to 6 bohr reproduces a smooth decay,
a sign flip, and partial recovery near $R=5$ bohr --- the signature of LiH's
ionic--covalent avoided crossing. The two backends agree to six decimals,
differing in overall sign only very near the crossing, where the phase is
inherently ambiguous.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item Only a single CAS block; general (GAS) restrictions are not handled,
though Malmqvist's Sec.~V covers them in principle.
\item Single pairs only. Plasser's overlap-matrix orthogonalization
(his Eqs.~(25)--(26)), which repairs displacement artifacts far better than
core freezing alone and diagnoses missing states via deviation from
orthogonality, needs an $N\times N$ matrix of state pairs and is not
implemented.
\item No CI-vector truncation. Plasser's Sec.~3.2 threshold, and the
renormalization of his Eq.~(20), would matter for expansions much larger than
CASSCF.
\item The exponential route costs a few times more sigma builds than
Malmqvist's exact LU algorithm; see \cref{sec:impl}.
\item Comparing a two-component wavefunction against a nonrelativistic one is
rejected, not supported.
\end{itemize}
